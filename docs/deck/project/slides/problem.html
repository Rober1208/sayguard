Construction sites and warehouses already have cameras, but few have anyone watching them live. The stakes are real: in 2025, 105 construction workers died at work in Taiwan, more than in any other industry, two in three of them from falls, according to the Ministry of Labor. Its institute's study of forklift accidents found that a person being struck by the truck is the most common kind, about thirty percent. And a person watching screens cannot keep up: a Sandia guide for the US Justice Department notes that after only twenty minutes, attention falls well below acceptable levels. So a missing hard hat is often noticed only in recorded footage, after an incident. Many AI cameras ship with a set list of rules, so a new zone, distance or time rule means a vendor project. And alarms that are often wrong tend to be ignored, while sending site video to the cloud raises privacy concerns.

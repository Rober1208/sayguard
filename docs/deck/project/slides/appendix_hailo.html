How the models share one Hailo-10H. The detector runs on every frame of every camera; for ten hard-hat cameras that is about forty percent of the chip by Hailo's reference speed. The CLIP encoder runs only when an alert needs checking, YOLO-World only on cameras whose rules name an object it alone knows, and the two language models only when someone speaks a rule or the daily report is due, one after the other, because Hailo's guide says vision and generative models can share the device but two generative models cannot run at once. Video over USB takes about a tenth of the link. The warehouse detector is already compiled for the chip at both shapes, in 3 and 4 parts because the network is too large for one context; fed frames in the cameras' 16:9 shape it keeps its accuracy in Hailo's emulator with 43 percent fewer pixels and operations. Hailo's profiler gives no throughput for multi-context builds, so what we cannot know before the device arrives is how fast the warehouse detector runs at either shape and whether one box carries twelve warehouse cameras, the cost of switching between those parts and between models, and how much the detector slows while the language model works; those are the first measurements of Stage II.
